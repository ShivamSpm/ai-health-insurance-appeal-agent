## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Medically Necessary

Health care services that a prudent provider would give a patient to prevent, diagnose or treat an illness, injury or its symptoms, and that are (a) in line with generally accepted standards of medical practice, (b) clinically appropriate in type, frequency, extent, site and duration, and (c) not mainly for the convenience of the patient or provider, and not more costly than an equally effective alternative.

### 2.2 Prior Authorization

Our approval, given before you receive a service, that the service is covered and medically necessary. Prior authorization is not a guarantee of payment if you are not eligible on the date of service.

### 2.3 Network and Out-of-Network Providers

A network provider has a contract with us to provide services to members at agreed rates. An out-of-network provider has no such contract.

### 2.4 Emergency Medical Condition

A condition with acute symptoms severe enough, including severe pain, that a prudent layperson with an average knowledge of health and medicine could reasonably expect the absence of immediate medical attention to place their health in serious jeopardy. We decide whether a condition was an emergency based on the symptoms, not the final diagnosis.

### 2.5 Adverse Benefit Determination

A denial, reduction or termination of, or failure to pay for, a benefit, including a decision that a service is not medically necessary, needed prior authorization, was out of network, or was billed incorrectly.

## 4. Covered Services

### 4.1 Preventive Care

Preventive services recommended by national guidelines are covered at no cost when you use a network provider. This includes screening colonoscopy starting at age 45. If a polyp is removed or a biopsy is taken during a screening colonoscopy, the procedure is still covered as preventive care with no cost sharing. The provider should identify it as a screening procedure on the claim (for example, with modifier PT or a screening diagnosis code).

### 4.2 Hospital and Surgical Services

Covered: inpatient hospital stays, surgery, anesthesia, and outpatient surgery when medically necessary. Elective admissions and outpatient surgery require prior authorization (Section 6.1).

### 4.3 Diagnostic Imaging and Testing

Covered: X-rays, laboratory tests, and advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET). Advanced imaging requires prior authorization except in an emergency.

### 4.4 Rehabilitation Therapy

Physical, occupational and speech therapy are covered up to 30 combined visits per calendar year. Visits after the 25th in a calendar year require prior authorization.

### 4.5 Prescription and Specialty Drugs

Drugs on our formulary are covered at the cost-sharing level shown in the formulary. Specialty drugs, including biologic drugs, require prior authorization and may be subject to step therapy (Section 5.2).

### 4.6 Durable Medical Equipment

Covered when prescribed by a provider and medically necessary, including continuous glucose monitors that meet the criteria in Section 5.3.

## 5. Medical Necessity

### 5.1 When We Cover a Service as Medically Necessary

We cover a service only if it is medically necessary as defined in Section 2.1. Our clinical reviewers decide medical necessity from the records your provider sends. If the records do not support the service, we may deny it. A denial for lack of medical necessity is made by a licensed clinician.

### 5.2 Conservative Treatment and Step Therapy

(a) **Spinal surgery**, including spinal fusion and implanting a spinal cord stimulator, is medically necessary only after at least six weeks of documented conservative treatment (such as physical therapy, medication or injections) has failed, unless there is a progressive neurological deficit.

(b) **Step therapy.** Biologic drugs for rheumatoid arthritis are covered only after a documented trial of methotrexate for at least 12 weeks, unless methotrexate is contraindicated or was not tolerated.

(c) **Reduction mammaplasty** is medically necessary when records document persistent symptoms (such as back, neck or shoulder pain, or skin breakdown) for at least six months despite conservative measures.

### 5.3 Clinical Criteria for Specific Services

(a) **Continuous glucose monitors** are covered for members with diabetes who use insulin, or who have documented problematic hypoglycemia (repeated low blood sugar episodes, or any episode needing help from another person).

(b) **In-facility sleep studies** are covered when a home sleep apnea test was inconclusive or technically inadequate, or when the member has a condition that makes home testing unreliable, such as moderate-to-severe lung disease, neuromuscular disease or heart failure.

(c) **Inpatient stays.** A continued hospital stay is medically necessary while the member needs services that can be given safely only in a hospital, such as intravenous antibiotics or supplemental oxygen that needs monitoring.

(d) **Rehabilitation therapy** is medically necessary while records show measurable functional improvement, or when therapy is needed to prevent significant loss of function.

### 5.4 Experimental or Investigational Services

We do not cover services that are experimental or investigational: services not approved by the FDA for the condition, or whose effectiveness is not supported by peer-reviewed evidence. Examples include stem cell injections for joint conditions and platelet-rich plasma injections. This exclusion applies even if your provider recommends the service.

## 6. Prior Authorization

### 6.1 Services That Require Prior Authorization

The following services require prior authorization under the Halcyon Ridge Gold EPO:

- Advanced imaging: MRI, CT, PET and nuclear cardiology studies
- Elective (non-emergency) inpatient admissions
- Outpatient surgery performed in a hospital outpatient department or ambulatory surgery center
- Specialty drugs, including infused and injected drugs given in a clinic or infusion center
- Sleep studies performed in a facility
- Physical, occupational and speech therapy after the 25th visit in a calendar year
- Genetic and molecular testing
- Bariatric surgery

Services not listed above do not require prior authorization. Emergency services never require prior authorization.

### 6.2 How Prior Authorization Works

Your provider requests prior authorization by phone, fax or our provider portal. We give each request a reference number. An approved authorization is valid for 90 days for the service approved. If the service moves to a different network facility, the authorization remains valid for the same service. If a provider can show that a request was submitted and approved, we will honor the approval even if it is missing from our records.

### 6.3 Urgent and Emergency Services

Emergency services do not need prior authorization. If a service that requires prior authorization was urgent and authorization could not reasonably be obtained beforehand, we will review the service for retrospective authorization. This review is available when your provider asks for it and on appeal. The records must show why the service could not wait.

### 6.4 When a Provider Does Not Obtain Prior Authorization

Network providers are responsible for obtaining prior authorization. If a network provider does not obtain a required authorization, we deny the claim to the provider, and the provider may not bill you for the denied amount. If an out-of-network provider does not obtain a required authorization, you are responsible for the cost.

## 7. Provider Network

### 7.1 Using Network Providers

You pay the least when you use network providers. Check the online provider directory or call Member Services to confirm a provider's network status. A provider's network status is tied to the provider and location listed in the directory.

### 7.2 Out-of-Network Coverage

This plan does not cover care from out-of-network providers, except as described in Sections 7.3 through 7.7 and 7.9. If you choose to see an out-of-network provider in any other situation, you pay the full cost.

### 7.3 Emergency Services

Emergency services are covered at the network cost-sharing level at any hospital emergency department, network or not, without prior authorization. Whether care was an emergency is decided by the symptoms (Section 2.4).

### 7.4 Network Access Exceptions

If no network provider of the needed type is available within 35 miles of your home, or cannot see you within 15 business days (10 business days for behavioral health), we will cover an out-of-network provider at the network cost-sharing level. You can ask for this before care or on appeal.

### 7.5 Continuity of Care

If your provider leaves our network while you are being treated for a serious and complex condition, or while you are in your second or third trimester of pregnancy, you may keep seeing that provider at the network cost-sharing level for up to 90 days, or through postpartum care for pregnancy.

### 7.6 Out-of-Network Providers at Network Facilities

If you get care at a network hospital, surgery center or provider's office, and an out-of-network provider you did not choose (such as an anesthesiologist, radiologist, pathologist or laboratory) is involved in your care, you pay only network cost sharing and cannot be balance billed.

### 7.7 Provider Directory Accuracy

If you got care from a provider who was listed as a network provider in our directory when you scheduled or received the care, and the listing was wrong, you pay no more than network cost sharing.

### 7.8 Referrals

You do not need a referral to see a network specialist.

### 7.9 Urgent Care While Traveling

Urgent care received more than 100 miles from your home is covered at the network cost-sharing level, even from an out-of-network provider.

## 8. Claims and Coding

### 8.1 Submitting Claims

Network providers submit claims for you. Claims must be submitted within 180 days of the date of service.

### 8.2 Coding Standards

Claims must use CPT, HCPCS and ICD-10-CM codes that are valid on the date of service and coded to the highest level of specificity. The place-of-service code must match where the service was performed. The units billed must match the quantity given, as defined in the code's unit description. While a provider corrects a coding error, you are not responsible for the denied amount.

### 8.3 Bundled and Same-Day Services

An office visit on the same day as a minor procedure is included in the procedure payment, unless the provider performed a significant, separately identifiable evaluation and reports it with modifier 25. Lab tests that are part of a panel are paid as the panel. A network provider may not bill you for services denied as bundled.

### 8.4 Duplicate Claims

We deny a claim line that duplicates a service already paid. A service done on both sides of the body, or more than once on the same day, is not a duplicate when it is billed with the right modifier (such as 50, RT/LT or 59).

### 8.5 Corrected Claims

A provider may submit a corrected claim within 180 days of the original denial. You may also appeal a coding denial yourself under Section 10.

## 9. Exclusions

### 9.1 General Exclusions

We do not cover: cosmetic services (except reduction mammaplasty that meets Section 5.2(c)); experimental or investigational services (Section 5.4); services not medically necessary; services received before coverage began or after it ended; and services for which you have no legal obligation to pay.

## 10. Appeals

### 10.1 Your Right to Appeal

You, or someone you authorize, may appeal any adverse benefit determination. A different reviewer from the one who made the original decision reviews your appeal.

### 10.2 Deadline to File an Internal Appeal

You must file an internal appeal within 90 calendar days after the date printed on the notice of adverse benefit determination. If the last day to file falls on a Saturday or Sunday, the deadline moves to the next Monday. This deadline is set by this plan document. If a notice states a different deadline, the deadline in this section applies.

### 10.3 What to Include With Your Appeal

Every appeal should include the claim number and a copy of the denial notice. Depending on the reason for the denial, also include:

- **Medical necessity denials:** Letter of medical necessity; Relevant medical records; Diagnostic test results
- **Prior authorization denials:** Physician's order for the service; Relevant medical records
- **Out-of-network denials:** Explanation of Benefits
- **Coding or billing denials:** Itemized bill; Corrected claim

Also include any document the denial notice says was missing.

### 10.4 Expedited Appeals

If waiting for a standard appeal could seriously harm your health, ask for an expedited appeal. We decide expedited appeals within 72 hours.

### 10.5 External Review

If we uphold our decision, you may ask for an independent external review within four months after the final internal appeal decision.

### 10.6 Where to Send Your Appeal

Mail: Halcyon Ridge Health Appeals Unit, P.O. Box 4410, Sacramento, CA 95812. You may also submit an appeal through the member portal or by calling 1-800-555-0142.
